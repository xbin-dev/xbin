# I2 — rollout measurement on the QA box

Work pack I2 (95-work-packs.md): measure the **default-partitioned** agent
on the QA box (deploy/qa) — 20 people, two instances — to set `E` and the
caps (PD-18, PD-35); verify that gocryptfs counts fall after the idle
unmount; verify the sealed-backup alert and the export on an upgraded
workspace; and D177 there. Branch `pt/i2` from master f6f0ba71 (= v0.3.66).
No D-number taken: fold at merge.

## The box and the run

- `ubuntu@84.239.100.188`: Ubuntu 26.04 amd64, 4 vCPU, MemTotal 7936 MiB
  (no swap), KVM. **xbind v0.3.66** (released 2026-10-01 18:58 UTC with
  partitions), system mode, `--isolate`, owner-token auth, vault
  auto-unsealed; upgraded in place from v0.3.65 (the updater) at 19:04 UTC.
  Backends run in namespace sandboxes (the agent declares no `"vm"`); this
  box's xbin.service has no per-backend cgroup leaves (every backend sits
  in `xbin.service/init`; the gocryptfs processes in the service root), so
  no backend had a memory cap — a pre-existing bug (§Bugs, 1).
- Baseline (19:21 UTC): 1 user (`admin`), instances `apps/agent` and
  `apps/agent2` (the owner's, unpartitioned, pre-D177), 3 backends running,
  5 gocryptfs mounts, 17 processes / 110 tasks in xbin.service, host
  "used" (MemTotal − MemAvailable) 765 MiB, xbind 41 MiB RSS.
- Run 19:21–21:07 UTC. Everything was driven on the box against
  127.0.0.1:8642: the owner token only inside remote commands (a 0700
  helper read it per call), people through `POST /api/xbin/login` (their
  own app session) and `GET /api/xbin/frame-token`, then the tile's API
  with `X-XBin-Frame-Token` — what a person's frame does.

## Method

1. `POST /templates/new {source: agent}` → `apps/qa-agent-a`,
   `apps/qa-agent-b`. Both answered `partition: ["user","global"]` and
   `GET /partitions` showed them `partitioned` at once (empty: PD-44's
   auto rule). 20 people `qa-p01`…`qa-p20` (role user, password, read on
   both instances).
2. "Opening the agent tab" as a person = a frame token, then `GET /me`
   (the cold start), `/conversations`, `/needs`, `/classes`. Each person
   opened both instances, sequentially, timed per request.
3. Measured with a script reading /proc as root (`snap.py`, every 30 s
   plus labelled snapshots): every process in xbin.service's cgroup,
   attributed by role — a backend by its `XBIN_COMPONENT`/`XBIN_PARTITION`
   (no other env read), a gocryptfs by its cipher dir, children (rootfs
   overlay, the gocryptfs logger) to their parent — with VmRSS and
   smaps_rollup's Pss; `/proc/mounts` fuse.gocryptfs; xbin.service's
   memory.stat and pids.current; host MemAvailable. Disk by `du -sb` and
   `GET /partitions` totals. A first start was decomposed with a 20 ms
   sampler (mounts, new processes, the partition's db bytes).
4. Two people per instance ran one short model turn (`POST /ask`,
   deepseek-v4-flash via the box's llm-gw, bound to the test instances
   only) to see what a conversation adds.
5. The default caps were measured first, then raised for the test tiles
   (`POST /partitions/limits`: workspace 64, each tile 25) to run all 40.

### The caps (PD-18) as measured

- Defaults on this box with the placeholder E = 160 MiB: **12 per tile,
  24 per workspace** (the API's `defaults`). Opening qa-agent-a for 20
  people one after another (~2.4 s each): p02…p13 started; p13's start **evicted
  p01** (idle > 2 min, LRU: "partition evicted" in the log); p14…p20 got
  the documented **503** `too many people's instances of apps/qa-agent-a
  are running; try again shortly` (93–151 ms).
- **Bug: a refused start leaves the person's volumes mounted for the idle
  hour.** p14…p20's refused starts each mounted two gocryptfs volumes —
  14 mounts, 28 processes, ~120 MiB RSS — kept until the idle unmount.
  Cause: the runner's gate (`partitionGate` → ShouldRunPartition, boot's
  composition with `PartitionEncryptionHoldReason` → `holdReasonIn` →
  `ensureVolume`) mounts before `admitPartition` refuses, and nothing
  unmounts. Repro: a partitioned tile with a file-backed resource, caps
  (`POST /partitions/limits {tile, maxRunning: 1}`), person A opens it,
  person B opens it within 2 min → 503, and `grep -c fuse.gocryptfs
  /proc/mounts` has grown by B's volumes, which stay for an hour. Every
  person a busy tile refuses adds two gocryptfs + two loggers — exactly
  when memory is short. A fix is written but **not shipped** (§Bugs 3):
  it only moves the unmount earlier, which frees nothing while sandboxes
  pin the views (§Bugs 2).

## E and the caps

**Shipped: E = 96 MiB** (was the 160 MiB placeholder). The measured
resident size of a person's agent partition is 55.8 MiB mean / 61.2 max;
96 keeps ~70% headroom over it for the heavier use this run didn't
measure (long conversations with large contexts, busy volumes — a short
model turn added 2 MiB, an active gocryptfs is ~4 MiB larger than an idle
one), because the value ships in v0.3.67 before the owner reviews it
(the coordinator: minimal and conservative). It is ~3.7× the marginal
host cost (26 MiB), and the caps spend only M/4 (per tile) and M/2
(workspace) of memory.

**Recommended to the owner: E = 64 MiB** — what the measurement alone
supports (the max RSS, rounded up; ~2.4× the marginal cost): even counted
by RSS at the max, the workspace cap's 62 partitions on this box would
take 3.7 GiB of its 3.9 GiB M/2, and by marginal cost 1.6 GiB. A
measurement of busy partitions (a long conversation, a coding agent's
session) should decide between 64 and 96.

| host M | E = 160 MiB (was) | **E = 96 MiB (shipped)** | E = 64 MiB (recommended) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 GiB | 4 / 8 | 4 / 8 | 4 / 8 |
| 2 GiB (a container) | 4 / 8 | 5 / 10 | 8 / 16 |
| 4 GiB (the macOS VM) | 6 / 12 | 10 / 21 | 16 / 32 |
| 7.75 GiB (this box) | 12 / 24 | 20 / 41 | 31 / 62 |
| 8 GiB | 12 / 25 | 21 / 42 | 32 / 64 |
| 16 GiB | 25 / 51 | 32 / 85 | 32 / 128 |
| ≥ 32 GiB | 32 / 102+ | 32 / 128 | 32 / 128 |

(per tile / per workspace.) The I2 load (20 people × 2 instances) now fits
under the defaults of this box (20/41); with the placeholder a team of 13+
opening one agent within 2 minutes met evictions and 503s. The clamps
(4–32 per tile, 8–128 workspace) stand. The other PD-18 constants stand as
measured: background starts ≤ 4 and mail ≤ 6/min weren't exercised
beyond their unit tests; the 10-min reap behaved as documented, and the
idle unmount did on the mount table but freed no daemon (below, §Bugs 2).

## Idle stop and idle unmount

**The idle stop (10 min) behaved as documented.** The reaper ran on its
minute tick: B's 20 partitions at 19:40:45 and A's 20 at 19:43:45, all of
a tile at once — 10 min after each tile's last restart (a binding change
restarted B's at 19:29:46 and A's at 19:33:05; a restart counts as use),
then A's p18–p20 at 19:56:45 (reopened 19:46:39) and p21 at 19:57:45. A
stopped partition keeps 4 processes (its two gocryptfs and loggers): 23.1
MiB RSS / 7.5 MiB PSS, ~9 MiB of host memory.

**The idle unmount took the views out of the mount table on time** —
`/proc/mounts` fuse.gocryptfs, logged every 5 s:

| time (UTC) | mounts | gocryptfs processes | what |
|---|---|---|---|
| 19:55:42 | 101 | 101 | 84 people's views + 17 tiles' |
| 20:41:33 | 61 | 101 | B's 40: 60 min 48 s after their 19:40:45 stop |
| 20:44:32 | 27 | 101 | A's 34 (p01–p17): 60 min 47 s after 19:43:45 |
| 20:48:31 | 61 | 135 | A's p01–p17 reopened: 34 new daemons |

— the documented hour after the stop, plus at most one disk-monitor pass
(45 s). **But the gocryptfs count never fell**: the 74 unmounted views'
daemons kept running, their FUSE connections alive with no mount in any
live namespace, and the reopen ran a second daemon on each of 34 cipher
dirs (§Bugs 2): every sandbox started while a view was mounted keeps a
copy of it; removing the mountpoint directory released one at once.

Cold start after the idle unmount (the views mounted again): 420–474 ms
one at a time (p01–p03); 14 at once (p04–p17, 4 vCPU): 1.13–1.64 s,
median 1.47 s.

## Sealed backups on the upgraded workspace

- Before: `GET /backup-keys` `{mode: sealed, keys: 0, unexported: 0,
  exports: 0}`, no `/alerts`, no archiver bound, no backup ever made. **So
  an upgraded workspace without an archiver shows no backup-keys alert** —
  as coded (`backupKeyAlerts`: none while there are no keys); the docs'
  "Until the first export, admins see an alert" reads as if it always
  shows. The barrier itself is in every bundle, so an operator relying on
  the nudge for disaster recovery of the *vault* gets none until a backup
  runs. Left as is; worth a sentence in 14-lifecycle.md (owner's call).
- A throwaway in-memory archiver (`apps/qa-i2-arch`, the isolated smoke
  suite's), bound `@archive` to qa-agent-a only. `POST /backup
  {component: apps/qa-agent-a}`: the first attempt raced the archiver's
  restart caused by its own new binding (gen 1 → 2 mid-PUT: 502 "use of
  closed network connection") — my timing, but it left 2 keys and the
  alert for a backup that has no archive; the retry: `{ok, partitions:
  {archived: 20}, version}` in 0.52 s.
- **The alert appeared as documented:** `/alerts` kind `backup-keys`,
  warn: "22 backup keys aren't in any export yet: restoring sealed backups
  on a new machine needs them — export a key bundle (bx backup keys
  export, or the admin console's Backup tab)" (tile, ns, 20 part keys).
- A partition archive at the archiver: `XBINSEAL`, header `{v:1, subkey:
  bk-…, chunk: 65536, kind: partition}`, no plaintext (no SQLite header,
  no person id).
- **The export works:** as a temporary admin person (`qa-i2-admin`, app
  session) `POST /backup-keys/export` → 200, 6.9 KB, `{schema: 1,
  workspace, created, barrier: {kdf, memory, salt, threads, time, version,
  wrapped_dek}, keys: [22 × {id, subject, tile, gen, created, schema,
  wrapped}], erased: []}`; the alert cleared; `GET /backup-keys`
  `unexported: 0`. Written into a 0700 temp dir on the box, inspected for
  shape only, then `shred -u`. The owner token may export too (it is an
  admin and no tile credential: `requireAdminPerson`), as documented.
- Then `POST /backup/erase {component, what: all}`: 22 keys tombstoned,
  "archiver … deleted 22 version(s)", `keys: 0`, no alert.

## Bugs and findings, by severity

None makes partitions unusable on a real install, so none blocks v0.3.67
by the release rule; 1 and 2 deserve fixes soon.

1. **HIGH, pre-existing, not fixed here: cgroup limits are silently off on
   system installs that auto-unseal with any encrypted file-backed
   resource.** On the box `GET /sandboxes` says `health.isolation.cgroup:
   false`, `xbin.service/cgroup.subtree_control` is empty, every backend
   sits in `xbin.service/init` with xbind, and the journal's last "cgroup
   v2 limits enabled" is 2026-09-26 06:18 — just before v0.3.60 made
   encrypted mounts work there (D110); 20 starts since, none enabled.
   Cause: boot's `vault` step (`UnsealOrInit` → `MountEncrypted`) forks
   the gocryptfs daemons while xbind is still in the service's root
   cgroup, and the later `cgroup` step (`cgroup.New`) moves only xbind
   into `init`, so enabling `+cpu +memory +pids` on the root fails with
   EBUSY (cgroup v2's no-internal-processes rule; the write's error is
   ignored, the read-back has no `memory`, `enabled` stays false). Effect:
   no per-component memory cap (`XBIN_LIMIT_MEM`, 2 GiB), no pids cap, no
   per-component (or per-partition) accounting — a runaway tile or person
   can take the host — on every such restart; people's-partition caps
   (E) are then the only memory bound on their instances. Repro: a system
   install with `XBIN_VAULT_MODE=auto` and a tile with a sqlite,
   filesystem or blob resource (any agent instance); `systemctl restart
   xbin`; `cat /sys/fs/cgroup/system.slice/xbin.service/cgroup.subtree_control`
   is empty and `…/xbin.service/cgroup.procs` lists the gocryptfs and
   logger pids. Fix (proposed): `cgroup.New` moves every process of the
   base into `init`, not only its own pid (or the `cgroup` step runs before
   `vault`). Not shipped tonight: it turns limits on at upgrade for every
   such install after a week without them — the owner's call.
2. **HIGH, partitions' idle unmount (PD-48): the unmount frees nothing,
   and the next start runs a second gocryptfs on the same ciphertext.**
   B's 40 views left the mount table at 20:41:33 and A's 34 at 20:44:32,
   on schedule, but `pgrep -c -x gocryptfs` stayed 101 and
   `/sys/fs/fuse/connections` 102: 74 FUSE connections with no mount in
   any live mount namespace (every process's and thread's mountinfo
   checked), no fd, cwd, root or mmap on them. Reopening A's p01…p17 then
   mounted 34 new daemons: **34 cipher dirs served by two gocryptfs at
   once** (e.g. `.partitions/apps~qa-agent-a/main/u-13a1…/fs/db`, pids
   519726 from 19:2x and 541137 from 20:48). Cause: a sandbox's mount
   namespace starts as a copy of xbind's, every gocryptfs view mounted at
   that moment included, and the copies stay for the sandbox's life —
   `internal/sandbox/init_linux.go` pivots and lazily detaches the old
   root, but in a user namespace the copied mounts are locked to their
   parents and fuse-overlayfs's own root (`fuseServerRoot`: a tmpfs stacked
   on that old root) keeps the detached tree alive; terminals carry the
   views in too, through their recursive workspace bind (09-terminals.md
   "Known limitation"). So every sandbox started while a person's view was
   mounted — globals, alwaysOn tiles, terminals, agent sessions, other
   people's partitions — pins it until it exits; here the owner's
   `apps/sandbox-terminal` backend (started 20:29:13) was the only sandbox
   alive across the unmounts. **Proved on the box:** (a) once every
   sandbox started since 20:48 had exited, resetting qa-p01's partition on
   A (which removes its mountpoint directories) let all four of its
   daemons go — its new pair and the pinned old pair; (b) then `rmdir` of
   one of B's unmounted, empty mountpoint directories
   (`.xbin/resenc/.partitions/apps~qa-agent-b/main/u-13a1…/fs/db`) made its
   pinned daemon (pid 521752) exit at once — the kernel detaches the
   mounts on a removed directory in every namespace. Effect: the idle
   unmount reclaims no memory while such a sandbox lives (~9 MiB a pair),
   and the next start doubles the daemons for that ciphertext. The pinned
   copy sits beyond every sandboxed process's root: a backend can't reach
   it (CapEff 0 and NoNewPrivs against an overlay process holding full
   capabilities in the same user namespace, seccomp, landlock), and in a
   terminal the `.xbin` mask shadows it and landlock withholds reads; the
   old daemon saw no I/O and nothing was corrupted. Repro: a partitioned
   tile with a file-backed resource; person A opens it; open any other
   tile or a terminal (a new sandbox, kept alive); after A's 10-min reap
   plus the idle hour, A's view leaves `/proc/mounts` but its gocryptfs
   keeps running; A opens the tile again → two gocryptfs on A's cipher
   dir. **Fix (proposed, one line, not shipped tonight):** in
   `resenc.UnmountIdle`, after a successful `fusermount -u`, remove the
   now-empty mountpoint directory (`os.Remove(mount)`; `Ensure` recreates
   it with `MkdirAll` before the next mount): (b) shows it frees the
   pinned daemon. It wants a FUSE + second-namespace test first (a copy
   held by an `unshare -m` child; unmount; rmdir; the daemon gone). Until
   then a restart of xbind clears them — the QA box's updater does that
   with v0.3.67.

## Recommendations (not done here)

- **Fix 2 (the pinned views: remove the mountpoint after the idle
  unmount), then ship 3** (`pt/i2-refused-volumes`).
- **E = 64 MiB** once a busy partition is measured (§E): 96 ships as the
  conservative value.
- **partitionIdle (PD-48, 60 min)**: once 2 is fixed, a stopped partition
  still keeps ~9 MiB (two gocryptfs + loggers, 4 processes, ~26 threads)
  for an hour after its 10-min reap, outside the caps; a remount costs
  ~0.1 s (the first-start decomposition; a cold start after the unmount
  took 0.42–0.47 s). Consider 15 min.
- **The agent template's fresh-schema migration** (1.7 s of a 2.4 s first
  start, through gocryptfs): one transaction for a new database.
- **The two loggers per partition** (`logger -t gocryptfs-<pid>-logger`,
  6 MiB RSS, 2 processes): gocryptfs's syslog redirection once it
  daemonizes (resenc runs it without `-fg`); `-nosyslog` with its stdio
  sent elsewhere would drop 2 of a partition's 6 processes — mind
  resenc's `CombinedOutput`, which waits on inherited pipes.
- Fix 1 (cgroups) and 5 (the token in the journal); the docs' "in
  development" status; the backup-keys nudge before the first backup.
